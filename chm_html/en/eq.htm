Equalizer and environmental models

The equalizer window covers everything from fine tonal adjustment to atmosphere — "as though you were hearing this in a large hall". There is no need to overthink it: start by picking a preset or a model and comparing by ear.

Equalizer with bands, environment, and presets

Start with a preset

There are 100 equalizer presets. Choosing one moves all fifteen sliders together, so hunting for something that matches your goal — more bass, clearer voices — is the fastest way in.

The 15-band equalizer

The spectrum is split into fifteen bands from low to high, each of which you can raise or lower.

  • Raise a slider and that band gets stronger; lower it and it recedes.
  • Overall level is set by the master, which is a separate path from the main volume.
  • Pushing bands up too far can make the sound clip. Shaping by cutting rather than boosting tends to go wrong less often.

Environmental models (100 of them)

One hundred models recreate the sense of hearing the music in a particular space, from a small room to a large hall.

  • The intensity of the effect is adjustable too, so you can apply just a touch of a space rather than all of it.
  • When the source is already loud, a dynamic compressor steps in automatically so the result does not clip.

Overall finishing

These work independently of the environmental models and apply across the whole sound.

Sharpness
Firms up the outlines of the sound.
Low / high balance
Shifts the balance between the bottom and the top end.
Sound density
Changes how tightly packed the sound feels.
Stereo depth
Changes how wide the image is.
Reverb / chorus / delay
Ambience, shimmer and echo. Push any of these sliders past the halfway point and they switch over to pan reverb, chorus distortion and multi delay.

Comparing with A/B snapshots

  1. Save a setting you like into A.
  2. Change the sliders and save a second setting into B.
  3. Switch between A and B while listening and keep the better one.

How this connects to other features

  • Turn on the EQ overlay in the Waveform and spectrum analyzer and the fifteen sliders are drawn as a line over the frequency view, so you can see exactly where the slider you are holding is taking effect.
  • The key-to-EQ suggestion in The companion tools proposes a preset based on the detected key of the track.
  • Playing with the DJ pad has a 3-band EQ plus Kill for low, mid and high.
  • Automating a performance with prompts can move EQ values over time.
Tip

To vary EQ per track, use Save per-song in Working with playlists. Starred tracks then play with their own settings.

Please note

The extended volume is yet another separate path from the main volume and the EQ master. Treat it as the last resort when nothing else is loud enough.
