The sound meter

This window shows the level coming into your microphone as relative dBFS, which makes it a convenient way to see how loud the room actually is.

Using it

  1. Choose the microphone to measure under Mic. Press Refresh if it is missing.
  2. The large figure and the level meter start moving.
  3. Hold keeps the loudest value reached.

The right-click menu

Response: fast
Follows fine detail. Good for catching sudden sounds.
Response: normal
The everyday setting.
Response: slow
Shows an average level, which suits judging the general noise of a room.
Clear peak hold
Discards the stored maximum so you can measure again.

When it comes in useful

  • Checking how much noise a room has before recording. Worth a look before The analog digitizer or Recording what you hear.
  • Moving a microphone around to find the quietest place to put it.
  • As a rough guide to whether your speakers are loud enough to bother the neighbours.
Please note

This is not a calibrated SPL meter. The reading is the relative level picked up by that particular microphone, and it changes with the microphone you use, so it cannot support a judgement like "that many decibels is fine". Where it does work is comparing before and after with the same microphone.

Tip

When recording, keep the meter off the very top. A little headroom leaves room to raise the level later.
