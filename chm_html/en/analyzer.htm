Waveform and spectrum analyzer

The analyzer is where you look at the sound rather than only hear it. The waveform and meters sit on top, frequency and stereo-field views below. Several display styles are available, so you can settle on one that feels familiar.

Waveform and frequency analyzer

The top half — waveform

The newest wave starts beside the level meters (and the balance meter), not under them. The visual start of the wave matches what you hear.

  • Scrolling wave — the wave flowing sideways. Change the speed from the right-click menu.
  • Envelope — a filled loudness shape, like a recording app.
  • Centre / width — the centre of the mix and the stereo spread, on two lanes.
  • Loudness history — average level with a simple guide line.
  • Frozen wave — a still view with the start of each wave lined up.
  • Afterglow wave — the same lining-up, with a fading trail.

Meters on the right

  • Level meters — average level plus a mark for the loudest moment. Hide them from right-click → Meter, or with M.
  • Left-right balance — whether the two sides are in step. Hiding the level meters does not hide this. With both on it sits to the left of the levels; with levels off it moves to the right edge.
  • The waveform scrolls from the left edge of whichever meters are showing.

The bottom half — frequency and stereo field

Pick these from right-click → Lower display. Soft 3D draws the same kinds.

  • Spectrum — lows to highs as a hill. Familiar looks (Ozone / Cubase / SPAN and so on) plus bars or a line.
  • Band bars — lows to highs in bands. Handy for checking how a room sounds.
  • Spectrogram — a map of lows to highs as time moves on.
  • Left-right motion — in step looks vertical; opposite looks sideways.
  • Width (density) — shows how wide the image is, or whether it leans to the centre.
  • Balance per band — for spotting a range where left and right disagree.
  • Offset per band — whether each range is in step or opposite.

Layouts

On the spectrum view the right-click menu offers overlay, split vertical, split horizontal, 2x2 and 2x4 — useful when you want to look at the channels of a multi-channel source one at a time.

The EQ overlay

The fifteen sliders from Equalizer and environmental models can be drawn over the frequency view. Seeing which peak the slider in your hand is affecting makes EQ work considerably easier.

Keyboard controls

KeyAction
WCycle the upper display kind
SCycle the lower display kind
MShow or hide the level meters (balance stays)
FFreeze the display
PToggle peak hold
EToggle the EQ overlay
Space or double-clickReset peaks

Reading values with the mouse

Hover over the display to see how high and how loud that spot is. On split layouts you get the values for the panel under the cursor.

Soft 3D

Right-click → Upper / Lower panel view switches a pane to Soft 3D. Drag to orbit, wheel to zoom, 0 to reset the camera. Wave, band bars and the width view all have matching 3D views.

Saving the view as a picture

The spectrogram jacket tool in The companion tools saves the analyzer view as an image — a nice way to keep the character of a track as a picture.

Tip

Moving the display is unlikely to interrupt the audio. Acrylic blur is supported here too. In acrylic mode the wave still scrolls from the left of the meters; the meter strip is overlaid rather than scrolled.

Please note

Right-clicking a track in the playlist opens the analyzer for it directly. Seeing the music on a piano roll opens the same way.
