The analog digitizer

This window records sound coming in from outside — a turntable, a cassette deck, a radio — as WAV, mp3 or FLAC. It includes tools for taming low-end rumble and the hiss you get during quiet passages.

Connecting and recording

  1. Connect your equipment to a PC input, such as line-in or an audio interface.
  2. Input — choose the input device. Press Refresh if it is missing.
  3. Choose Format and Quality.
  4. Set the Save path.
  5. Play something and watch the In meter, adjusting Gain until it stops pinning to the top.
  6. Press Start recording. Press again to stop.

Three knobs for cleaning things up

HPF (high-pass filter)
Cuts the very bottom end. Use it against turntable rumble or vibration coming up through the floor.
Gain
Matches the incoming level. Turn it down whenever the meter clips.
Gate
Cuts very quiet sound, which makes the hiss between tracks much less noticeable. Applied too strongly it will eat the tail of a fade, so keep it modest.

Listening while you record

Choose an output under Monitor and tick Monitor on. Processed 16-bit stereo audio comes back to you, which lets you hear exactly what the HPF and Gate are doing.

After recording

  • To split one long recording into tracks, drop cues with Cues, A-B and practice tempo and cut them out with the A-B / cue pack export in The companion tools.
  • Add titles and other information with Editing tags.
  • To even out levels, use Tools for shaping the sound or the loudness normalize batch.
Tip

Always make a short test recording before the real one. Levels slightly on the quiet side, with headroom on the meter, are the safe choice.

Please note

mp3 and FLAC are converted through a temporary WAV, so expect a short wait after stopping.
