Smart playlists and the library tree

Once a collection grows, arranging it by hand becomes a chore. A smart playlist fills itself with whatever matches the rule you set. The library (Lib) tree gives you the whole collection as a folder tree you can browse.

Creating a smart playlist

  1. Open the smart playlist dialog from the playlist right-click menu.
  2. Set your conditions. Artist, album and star rating can be combined.
  3. Give it a name and save.
  4. From then on, matching tracks appear automatically — adding music needs no further work from you.

For example, "three stars or more" plus "artist contains Falcom" gives you a list of nothing but your favourite game music.

The library tree

Register the folder that holds your music as a root and everything beneath it appears as a tree.

  • Add root registers a top-level music folder.
  • PC nodes let you browse folders on your computer directly.
  • Smart-PL nodes place your smart playlists inside the tree.

You can send tracks from the tree into a playlist, which makes hunting down "that one track on that one album" much faster.

Missing heat and cleanup

Places in the library where files have gone missing are shaded as "missing heat" — the darker the shading, the more is gone. Unplugging an external drive or moving folders will do it. The manage function lets you fix or remove them in bulk.

Tip

Star ratings are set from the right-click menu in Working with playlists. The more you rate, the more useful smart playlist conditions become.

Please note

A smart playlist arranges itself from its rule, so reordering rows inside one by hand is not saved. When the order matters, use an ordinary playlist or a temporary playlist.
