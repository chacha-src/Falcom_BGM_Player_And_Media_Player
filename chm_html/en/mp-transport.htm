Playing, stopping and volume

This is the part you touch every day. Most of it does exactly what it looks like, but the combination of continuous play and loop count is worth a moment's explanation.

The basic buttons

Play / pause
Plays when stopped, pauses when playing. A pause remembers the position, so pressing again picks up where you left off.
Stop
Ends playback and returns to the start. If Saving to WAV is running, stopping finalises the file.
Previous / next
Moves through the playlist order. With Continuous-play crossfade on, the handover to the next track is smooth.
Volume
The overall level. It is a separate path from the equalizer master and the extended volume, so if raising it is not enough, check Equalizer and environmental models as well.

Random play

Tick this and tracks play in a shuffled order rather than in list order. If you want to decide what comes next, put it in the Up Next queue in Working with playlists — that takes priority.

Continuous play

Moves on to the next track automatically when one ends. Untick it and playback stops after a single track. Continuous play is also the prerequisite for Continuous-play crossfade, which overlaps the end of one track with the start of the next.

Loop count

For tracks that carry loop points — game music, typically — you can say how many times to loop before moving on.

  • One loop gives you the whole track once, then the next one.
  • A higher count keeps the same track going for a while.
  • For repeating just part of a track, use A-B. See Cues, A-B and practice tempo.

Letting Raira remember per-track settings

Tick Save per-song and tracks marked with a star reload their saved volume and equalizer when they play. When you untick it, Raira first saves the current settings to that track, then switches the feature off.

Tip

Right-clicking the taskbar icon opens a jump list with recently played tracks plus replay, stop, previous and next. Hovering the taskbar button gives you a thumbnail with small buttons of its own. See Shortcuts and the command palette.
