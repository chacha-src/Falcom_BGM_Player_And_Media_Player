Playing with the DJ pad

The DJ pad is for working the sound with your hands while it plays. There is nothing to configure — press a button and the sound changes at once. Good fun on its own, and genuinely useful on a stream.

DJ pad

Opening it

Use the DJ button on the media player's bottom row.

What is on the pad

Pitch and tempo
Quick moves for speed and pitch, hitting the same controls as Changing tempo and pitch.
Vinyl scratch
The sound of dragging a record. Good for punctuating a track change.
Kill (low / mid / high)
Drops a whole band out at once — cut the low end and everything suddenly hollows out, then slams back when you release.
Beat nudge
Shifts by a beat at a time. It lands nicely when the tempo is known, so measure it in Measuring BPM first.
Hot cues
Jump instantly to a set point. Works with the cues you dropped in Cues, A-B and practice tempo.
A-B
Loops a range, which lets you keep a phrase circling while you build on it.
Vocal and width
Pull the voice out or push it forward, and change the stereo width. Same job as Tools for shaping the sound.
Filter, FX and speed
Muffle the sound, apply an effect, or change the rate.
3-band EQ
Rough control over low, mid and high. For precise work use Equalizer and environmental models.

Getting into the next track

The transition presets in The companion tools apply combinations of EQ sweep, filter and crossfade duration aimed at the next track.

To line up a track in a compatible key, the key-detect and Camelot compatibility tool lists the candidates for you.

Using keys and knobs

Controlling Raira from a MIDI keyboard lets you assign these controls to notes and CC on your own hardware, and MIDI learn ties a knob to tempo or an EQ band.

From your phone

Controlling Raira from a phone or PC has a DJ scratch tab with a record you drag with your finger — handy when you are away from the keyboard.

Tip

If you would rather stumble across these effects than press them, the orbs in Bonus: the Soft3D aerial race and Bonus: the Soft3D maze change tempo and pitch as you play.

Please note

Leaving Kill or a filter engaged and forgetting about it is the classic way to convince yourself something is broken. The reset controls always take you back to the plain sound.
