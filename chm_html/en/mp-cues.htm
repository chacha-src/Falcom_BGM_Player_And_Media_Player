Cues, A-B and practice tempo

"I want to jump straight to the chorus." "I want these four bars on repeat." "This is too fast — play it slower." These three features cover all of that, and they suit instrument practice as much as DJ-style play.

Dropping cues

  1. Play up to the spot you want to return to.
  2. Right-click the seek bar and choose to add a cue. The cue list in Playback details can add them too.
  3. Press 1 to 8 to jump to your markers.

Cues are remembered per track, so they are still there the next time you open that file.

Repeating with A-B

Set an A point and a B point and playback repeats between them.

  • Press R to build an A-B range around the current position — handy because you can hit it the instant you think "that bit".
  • The seek bar's right-click menu lets you place A and B precisely.
  • Drag the blue thumbs to widen or narrow the range.
  • With a lyrics file loaded, the cyan markers on the seek bar make ready-made A-B candidates. See Showing lyrics.

Practice tempo

Slow the track down without changing the pitch, which is exactly what you want when working out a phrase by ear.

  • One-press presets at 50%, 75% and 100%.
  • Continuous adjustment across the 50–200% range.
  • To move the pitch as well, see Changing tempo and pitch.

Turning cues and ranges into files

Your cues and A-B ranges can be cut straight out as audio. The A-B / cue pack export in The companion tools writes each range as a numbered file.

Tip

For instrument practice, pair this with Tuner practice. Its Phrase A-B button builds a practice range around the current position on the spot.

Please note

The count after L tells you how many A-B passes have gone by. The repeat never stops on its own, so clear the A-B range or skip to the next track when you are done.
