The bonus games

Raira includes a couple of diversions for while you listen. They sit entirely apart from the playback features, but what makes them interesting is that playing them changes the tempo and volume of the music.

The two of them

Bonus: the Soft3D maze
A first-person DirectX 11 maze running from the ground floor down to three basement levels, each with its own wall and floor motifs. Walls and floors use photo textures embedded in the exe. Touching a floating orb changes an audio setting. Footsteps, doors and stairs use synthesized PCM effects.
Bonus: the Soft3D aerial race
A race flown along a ribbon of light. Each theme has real terrain — hills, valleys, rivers and carved mountain tunnels — and the ground uses photo textures embedded in the exe. Item orbs affect both the music and the race. Engine notes are PCM-synthesized and pan with distance.

Both open from the Maze and Race buttons on the media player's bottom row.

How they connect to the music

Both games scatter items that change playback settings when you touch them.

  • Tempo up and down (Changing tempo and pitch)
  • Pitch up and down
  • Previous and next track (Playing, stopping and volume)
  • Volume up and down
  • Change the EQ, or flatten it (Equalizer and environmental models)
  • Apply reverb
  • Toggle crossfade (Continuous-play crossfade)
  • Toggle random play

Which items appear is chosen from the right-click menu, so if you want to play without the music changing, narrow the item types down.

Other things to look at

  • The screensaver-style visualizer in The companion tools fills the screen with a display that moves with the music. ESC exits.
  • The photo frame shows pictures while you listen.
  • The Waveform and spectrum analyzer and Seeing the music on a piano roll are not games, but they are the tools for watching the music.
Please note

The maze and the race cannot be open at the same time. Both use DirectX 11, so if initialisation fails, try updating your graphics driver.

Tip

Both windows have a "?" button explaining the controls and how to read the display, with a legend. Worth a look the first time you open either one.
