Working with playlists

A playlist holds your tracks, but more than that it is the way in to everything you might do to a track. Right-click a row and you get the lot, from tag editing to export.

Playlists appear in the lower part of the main screen

Adding tracks

  • Drag files or whole folders in from Explorer.
  • Coming from another program? See Importing playlists from other software.
  • Want a list that fills itself from a rule? See Smart playlists and the library tree.

Sorting and moving

  • Click a column header to sort by it.
  • Selected tracks can be moved or copied to another playlist.
  • A temporary playlist lets you gather what you feel like hearing right now while leaving the original list untouched.
  • Tracks put in the Up Next queue play next regardless of the current order, and you can reorder the queue itself.

The marks on a row

Note icon
The track playing now. It blinks.
Heart
The track currently selected.
Star
This track has per-song settings saved (volume, EQ and so on).
Missing mark
The file cannot be found.
Mark-column chips (amber SAV / blue LRC / green MONO·LR·2.1…)
They appear near the name or in the Mark column. Amber means per-song settings, blue means lyrics (.lrc) are present, and green is the channel count. WAV and FLAC can be read from the header when the list is drawn; VST MIDI, m4a and similar formats get their mark once playback (or a Continuous-play crossfade soft-open) has confirmed the channel count.

Tidying up

Missing-file review
Lists tracks that cannot be found so you can fix paths, apply a fix to several at once, or edit inline. Rescan afterwards to clear the marks.
Duplicates dialog
Finds tracks that appear twice so you can clean the list up.
Folder sync lists
Keeps a playlist in step with the contents of a folder, so dropping new music into the folder adds it to the list.
Star ratings
Rate tracks and then use those ratings as conditions in Smart playlists and the library tree.
Tip

To vary volume or EQ per track, use Save per-song. Starred tracks come back with their saved settings the next time they play.

Please note

Video rows and unplayable entries are skipped automatically during continuous play, so you can keep music and video in one list and still let the music run. To watch a video properly, see Playing video.
